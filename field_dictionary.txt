================================================================================
STOCK SCREENER — OUTPUT FIELD DICTIONARY
================================================================================

This file defines every column present in the downloadable CSV output from the
Stock Screener.  Fields are grouped by category.  Each entry includes a plain-
language definition and practical notes for investors who are newer to these
metrics.

Data is sourced from two places:
  • Fundamental fields  →  local SQLite database (refreshed quarterly via the
                            Fundamentals Refresh page or the CLI script)
  • Technical fields    →  computed from 5-year daily OHLCV history fetched
                            from Yahoo Finance and cached locally

All indicator values reflect the MOST RECENT trading day in the cached history.

================================================================================
SECTION 1: IDENTIFIERS
================================================================================

ticker
  The exchange ticker symbol for the stock (e.g. AAPL, MSFT).
  Source: user input.

company_name
  The full legal or commonly used company name (e.g. "Apple Inc.", "Microsoft
  Corporation").
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: longName).
  Use: Purely informational — makes results easier to read without looking up
  tickers.  Not used as a filter criterion.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: COMPANY CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

sector
  The broad economic sector the company belongs to (e.g. Technology, Energy,
  Healthcare).
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Sector filters let you focus a screen on industries you follow or
  diversify across sectors you're underweight in.

industry
  A more granular classification within a sector (e.g. "Consumer Electronics"
  within Technology, "Oil & Gas Integrated" within Energy).
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Useful for peer comparisons — screening within a single industry removes
  noise from cross-sector valuation differences.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: VALUATION METRICS
================================================================================

market_cap
  Total market capitalisation: share price × shares outstanding.  Displayed
  with abbreviations (e.g. 1.2T, 450B, 50M).  Raw CSV value is in USD.
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: A rough proxy for company size.  Large-cap (>$10B) stocks tend to be
  more liquid and stable; small-cap (<$2B) stocks can be more volatile but may
  offer higher growth potential.  Many institutional investors are restricted
  to a minimum market cap.

trailing_pe  (Trailing Price-to-Earnings)
  Share price divided by actual earnings per share over the past 12 months.
  A P/E of 20 means investors are paying $20 for each $1 of current earnings.
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Compare within sectors — a P/E of 30 is normal in high-growth tech but
  expensive for a utility.  Negative P/E means the company had a net loss;
  the "exclude negative" filter is useful when you only want profitable companies.
  Rule of thumb: lower P/E may indicate undervaluation, but also check growth
  prospects before concluding a stock is "cheap."

forward_pe  (Forward Price-to-Earnings)
  Share price divided by analyst consensus EPS estimates for the next 12 months.
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Forward P/E reflects market expectations of future profitability.  A
  forward P/E well below trailing P/E implies the market expects strong earnings
  growth.  Treat with caution — analyst estimates can be wrong.

price_to_book  (Price-to-Book Ratio)
  Share price divided by book value per share (total assets minus total
  liabilities).  A P/B of 1.0 means the stock trades at exactly its accounting
  net asset value.
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: More meaningful for capital-intensive sectors (banks, insurance,
  manufacturing) than for asset-light technology companies.  P/B < 1 can signal
  deep value or financial distress — always investigate the reason.

peg_ratio  (Price/Earnings-to-Growth Ratio)
  Trailing P/E divided by the company's expected annual earnings growth rate
  (in percent).  A PEG of 1.0 means the P/E equals the growth rate — often
  considered "fairly valued."
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: pegRatio).
  Use: Adjusts P/E for growth, making it easier to compare a fast-growing
  company against a slow grower.  PEG < 1 may indicate undervaluation relative
  to growth; PEG > 2 suggests the market is pricing in very optimistic
  expectations.  Not meaningful if earnings or growth estimates are negative.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: RISK & INCOME
================================================================================

beta
  A measure of the stock's price volatility relative to the broad market
  (typically the S&P 500).  Beta = 1.0 means the stock historically moves in
  line with the market.
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Beta > 1 → amplified market moves (higher risk, potentially higher
  reward).  Beta < 1 → more muted moves (defensive).  Beta < 0 is rare and
  means the stock tends to move against the market (e.g. some gold miners).
  Useful for portfolio construction: adding low-beta stocks reduces overall
  portfolio volatility.

dividend_yield
  Annual dividend per share divided by the current share price, expressed as a
  percentage (e.g. 0.015 stored as 1.50% in the display).
  Source: fundamentals DB.
  Use: Income investors screen for a minimum yield.  Very high yields
  (>8–10%) can indicate dividend risk — the market may be pricing in a coming
  cut.  Always check the payout ratio (dividends ÷ earnings) to gauge
  sustainability.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: ANALYST RATINGS
================================================================================

recommendation_key
  The analyst consensus rating as a text label.  Common values:
    strongBuy   — majority of analysts rate as a strong buy
    buy         — consensus leans toward buying
    hold        — neutral; analysts see limited near-term upside or downside
    underperform— mild negative; expected to lag the market
    sell        — analysts expect the price to decline
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: recommendationKey).
  Use: A quick qualitative filter.  Combine with recommendation_mean for a
  more nuanced view.  Note that analyst ratings lag price moves and can be
  biased by investment banking relationships.

recommendation_mean
  Analyst consensus rating on a numeric scale: 1.0 (Strong Buy) → 5.0 (Sell).
  Most stocks cluster between 1.5 and 3.5.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: recommendationMean).
  Use: Screening for mean < 2.0 targets stocks where the analyst community
  is predominantly bullish.  Combine with number_of_analyst_opinions to
  filter out thinly covered stocks where one or two outlier ratings dominate
  the average.

number_of_analyst_opinions
  The number of analysts contributing to the consensus rating.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: numberOfAnalystOpinions).
  Use: A minimum analyst coverage filter (e.g. >= 5) removes micro-cap stocks
  with sparse coverage where the consensus mean is statistically unreliable.
  Institutional-grade coverage is generally considered >= 10 analysts.

target_median_price
  The median price target set by analysts covering the stock.  Represents the
  midpoint of analyst price forecasts for the next 12 months.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: targetMedianPrice).
  Use: Compared against the current close price to compute upside_to_target.
  The median is more robust than the mean against outlier estimates.

upside_to_target  (Derived field — computed at screen time)
  Formula: (target_median_price − Close) / Close × 100
  Positive value = stock is trading below the analyst consensus target
  (potential upside).  Negative value = stock already trades above target.
  Source: computed from target_median_price (fundamentals DB) and Close
  (technical data).
  Use: Screen for stocks with meaningful analyst-implied upside (e.g. >= 15%).
  Combine with recommendation_key = "buy" or "strongBuy" for a conviction
  filter.  Remember that price targets are updated infrequently and may not
  reflect very recent news.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: OWNERSHIP & SHORT INTEREST
================================================================================

held_percent_insiders
  The percentage of shares outstanding held by corporate insiders (executives,
  directors, and large shareholders classified as insiders under SEC rules).
  Stored as a decimal (0.05 = 5%); displayed as a percentage.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: heldPercentInsiders).
  Use: High insider ownership (>20%) suggests management has skin in the game —
  often seen as a positive alignment signal.  Very low insider ownership may
  not be a concern for large-cap companies but can be a flag for smaller ones.

held_percent_institutions
  The percentage of shares outstanding held by institutional investors (mutual
  funds, ETFs, pension funds, hedge funds, etc.).
  Stored as a decimal (0.65 = 65%); displayed as a percentage.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: heldPercentInstitutions).
  Use: High institutional ownership (>70%) indicates widespread institutional
  confidence and tends to provide price stability.  Screening for rising
  institutional ownership (across quarterly snapshots) can surface stocks
  gaining institutional interest.

short_percent_of_float
  The percentage of the float (shares available for public trading) currently
  sold short.  Stored as a decimal (0.05 = 5%); displayed as a percentage.
  Source: fundamentals DB (Yahoo Finance field: shortPercentOfFloat).
  Use: High short interest (>15–20%) can mean the market is broadly bearish on
  the stock — contrarian investors sometimes screen for high-short-interest
  stocks expecting a short squeeze.  It can also mean informed traders have
  identified fundamental problems.  Low short interest (<2%) generally
  indicates the market is not anticipating significant problems.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: PRICE & VOLUME (TECHNICAL)
================================================================================

Close
  The unadjusted closing price on the most recent trading day in the cached
  history.  Note: this is the raw close, not the split- or dividend-adjusted
  close.  Indicator math is performed on the same unadjusted series for
  consistency.
  Source: Yahoo Finance OHLCV history.

Volume
  The number of shares traded on the most recent trading day.
  Source: Yahoo Finance OHLCV history.
  Use: Absolute volume is less useful than relative volume (see RVOL_50 below).
  Spikes in volume often precede or accompany significant price moves.

================================================================================
SECTION 8: MOMENTUM & VOLATILITY INDICATORS (TECHNICAL)
================================================================================

RSI_14  (Relative Strength Index, 14-period)
  A momentum oscillator ranging from 0 to 100 that measures the speed and
  magnitude of recent price changes.  Calculated over the last 14 trading days.
  Commonly used thresholds: <30 = oversold, >70 = overbought.
  Source: computed via pandas-ta on full 5-year history.
  Use: Screen for oversold stocks (RSI < 30) for mean-reversion setups, or
  strongly trending stocks (RSI > 50 and rising) for momentum strategies.
  RSI divergence (price makes a new high but RSI does not) can warn of trend
  exhaustion.

RVOL_50  (Relative Volume vs. 50-day Average)
  Today's volume divided by the 50-day simple moving average of volume.
  RVOL = 1.0 means today's volume is exactly average; RVOL = 2.0 means twice
  the average.
  Source: computed from Yahoo Finance volume data.
  Use: Elevated RVOL (>1.5) during a price breakout confirms institutional
  participation.  High RVOL on a down day may indicate distribution (selling
  pressure).  Useful for momentum and breakout screens.

Pct_of_252_High  (Price as % of 52-week High)
  Close / rolling 252-trading-day High.  A value of 0.98 means the stock is
  2% below its 52-week high.
  Source: computed from Yahoo Finance OHLCV.
  Displayed as a percentage (e.g. 95.3%).
  Use: Screen for breakout candidates (Pct_of_252_High >= 97–100%) — stocks
  hitting new 52-week highs often continue upward.  Alternatively, screen for
  deeply depressed stocks (<50%) for deep-value or turnaround plays.

Pct_of_252_Low  (Price as % of 52-week Low)
  Close / rolling 252-trading-day Low.  A value of 1.30 means the stock is
  30% above its 52-week low.
  Source: computed from Yahoo Finance OHLCV.
  Displayed as a percentage (e.g. 130.0%).
  Use: Complements Pct_of_252_High.  A value near 1.0 means the stock is near
  its 52-week low — could signal distress or an entry opportunity depending on
  context.

SECTION 9: MOVING AVERAGES (TECHNICAL)
================================================================================

Moving averages smooth out day-to-day price noise and help identify trends.
When price is above a moving average, it generally indicates an uptrend for
that timeframe; below indicates a downtrend.

SMA_5   (5-period Simple Moving Average of Close)
  Average of the last 5 closing prices.  Responds very quickly to price changes.
  Use: Short-term trend / noise filter.  Traders watch for price to hold above
  SMA_5 as a sign of near-term strength.

SMA_50  (50-period Simple Moving Average of Close)
  Average of the last 50 closing prices (~10 weeks).
  Use: Intermediate-term trend.  The 50-day MA is widely watched; a cross of
  the 50-day MA by the 200-day MA is called the "Golden Cross" (bullish) or
  "Death Cross" (bearish).  Price holding above SMA_50 is a common institutional
  screening criterion.

SMA_100 (100-period Simple Moving Average of Close)
  Average of the last 100 closing prices (~5 months).
  Use: Intermediate-to-long-term trend support/resistance level.

SMA_200 (200-period Simple Moving Average of Close)
  Average of the last 200 closing prices (~10 months).
  Use: The most widely watched long-term trend indicator.  Many institutional
  investment rules require a stock to be above its 200-day MA.  Price
  crossing above the 200-day MA after a prolonged decline is often seen as a
  major bullish signal.  NaN for stocks with less than 200 trading days of
  history.

EMA_20  (20-period Exponential Moving Average of Close)
  A 20-day moving average that weights recent prices more heavily than older
  ones, making it react faster than a simple MA of the same length.
  Use: Popular in momentum strategies.  EMA_20 is often used as a dynamic
  support/resistance level in trending markets.  A stock consistently bouncing
  off its EMA_20 is considered to be in a healthy uptrend.

SECTION 10: BOLLINGER BANDS (TECHNICAL)
================================================================================

Bollinger Bands consist of a middle band (20-day SMA) and two outer bands set
2 standard deviations above and below the middle.  They expand during volatile
periods and contract during quiet ones.

BB_upper  (Upper Bollinger Band, 20-period, 2 std dev)
  The upper boundary of the band.  Price touching or exceeding this level can
  indicate the stock is extended (overbought in the short term) or is in a
  strong trend (a "Bollinger squeeze" breakout).
  Source: computed via pandas-ta.

BB_middle  (Middle Bollinger Band = SMA_20)
  The 20-day simple moving average — the centre of the band.  Often acts as
  a support/resistance level during trending markets.
  Source: computed via pandas-ta.

BB_lower  (Lower Bollinger Band, 20-period, 2 std dev)
  The lower boundary.  Price at or below BB_lower can indicate the stock is
  oversold or experiencing a sharp breakdown.
  Source: computed via pandas-ta.
  Combined use: A common setup is to screen for stocks where Close < BB_lower
  (oversold pullback in an otherwise uptrending stock) combined with RSI_14
  < 35 for a mean-reversion entry.  Conversely, Close > BB_upper + RSI_14 > 70
  may signal an extended short-term move.

================================================================================
NOTES FOR NEW INVESTORS
================================================================================

1. No single metric tells the full story.  Screens should be used to narrow
   a large universe down to a shortlist for deeper research — not as a
   buy/sell signal in isolation.

2. Fundamental data is refreshed quarterly.  Prices and technical indicators
   reflect the most recent cached trading day.  Make sure your fundamentals DB
   is current before a screen that relies heavily on fundamental filters.

3. NaN (missing) values appear as "—" in the display table and as empty cells
   in the CSV.  A missing value typically means insufficient history
   (e.g. a recent IPO) or that Yahoo Finance does not report the field for
   that ticker.  The filter step automatically excludes rows where a filtered
   column is NaN.

4. upside_to_target is a derived field — it depends on the analyst consensus
   price target, which is itself an estimate.  Treat it as a rough sentiment
   indicator rather than a precise forecast.

5. short_percent_of_float data from Yahoo Finance can lag by 2–4 weeks
   (short interest is reported twice monthly).  Check the as_of_date column
   to understand the age of the snapshot you are working with.
